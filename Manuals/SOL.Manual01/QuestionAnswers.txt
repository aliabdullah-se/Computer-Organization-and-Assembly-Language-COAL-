Q: What is the opcode of the instruction MOV AX, someConstant?
A: B80500   

Q: Verify the above opcode everywhere this instruction has been used in the program.
A: Verified

Q: Identify and verify the opcode of MOV BX, someConstant throughout the machine
code.
A: BB0A00          

Q: What is the offset of the first instruction?
A: 00000000 

Q: Why are the offsets of the second and third instructions separated by 3 bytes?
A: The Ofsets of 2nd 3rd instructions are separated by 3 bytes because these instructions are consuming 6 nibbles of memory in hexadecimal.
 
AFD - DEBUGGER Questions:

Q: What is the value of the IP (Instruction Pointer) register when the program is initially
loaded? What does this value indicate?
A: Instruction Pointer (IP) contains the offset/address of the next instruction to be
executed. For a .COM program, the first instruction begins at offset 0100h because the
first 256 bytes are occupied by the DOS Program Segment Prefix. Therefore, when you
see the initial IP in AFD, relate it to [ORG 0x0100].

Q: Record the initial values of the data registers AX, BX, CX & DX.
A: AX:0000
   BX:0000
   CX:0000
   DX:0000
